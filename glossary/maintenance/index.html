Inspection & Surveys

Roof Inspection
Roof inspection is a visual or physical assessment of a roof's condition, covering tiles, flashings, ridge and hip caps, gutters, soffits, and fascias to identify defects, deterioration, or water ingress risk. Inspections may be carried out from ground level using binoculars, from a ladder, or from the roof surface itself. For Metrotile steel tile roofs, annual inspections are recommended and typically reveal little remedial work given the coating's durability; the main focus is on mortar-free accessories, valley linings, and flashings rather than the tiles themselves. Inspections should be documented with photographs for future reference and to support any guarantee claims.

Roof Survey
Roof survey is a more formal, often professionally conducted assessment that produces a written report with findings, photographs, condition ratings, and recommended remedial works — typically required for insurance claims, building sales, refurbishment planning, or housing stock assessments. A Level 2 or Level 3 RICS HomeBuyer or Building Survey will include the roof, and specialist roofing surveyors may be commissioned separately for commercial or large-scale residential schemes. Metrotile's 40-year guarantee and BBA certification simplify the surveying process since the product's long-term performance credentials are independently verified.

See also: Roof Inspection · Thermographic Survey

Thermographic Survey
Thermographic survey (also called infrared survey) uses thermal imaging cameras to detect heat loss, moisture ingress, and insulation deficiencies in a roof. Wet insulation retains heat differently from dry insulation; missing or compressed insulation creates cold spots visible in IR imagery. Thermographic surveys are particularly valuable for diagnosing subtle leaks, poor loft insulation, and cold bridging without invasive opening-up work. They are most effective at night or in cold weather when the temperature differential between interior and exterior is greatest. Some local authorities and housing associations use aerial thermographic surveys across entire estates to prioritise retrofit programmes.

Also called: Infrared Roof Survey · IR Survey

See also: Leak Detection · Drone Survey

Drone Survey
Drone survey uses an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) equipped with high-resolution optical or thermal cameras to inspect roofs at close range without erecting scaffolding or access equipment. Drone surveys are particularly cost-effective for large or complex roofs, multi-storey buildings, or roofs over inaccessible areas. The UAV operator must hold a Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) A2 Certificate of Competency or PFCO, and operations near airports or controlled airspace require additional authorisation. Drone imagery can be combined with photogrammetry software to produce 3D point clouds and precise area measurements, useful for re-roofing quotations and stock condition surveys.

Also called: UAV Survey · Aerial Roof Inspection

Leak Detection
Leak detection is the process of locating the source of water ingress into a building from the roof. Because water can travel along rafters or sarking felt some distance from its entry point before dripping into the interior, identifying the true origin of a leak often requires systematic investigation: checking flashings, ridge joints, valley linings, and any penetrations (vents, skylights, aerials). Electronic leak detection methods — including low-voltage and high-voltage spark testing — are used on flat roofs and waterproof membranes. For pitched roofs, tracer dye, water-flow testing, or thermal imaging are common diagnostic tools. Prompt leak detection prevents secondary damage to insulation, timberwork, and internal finishes.

See also: Thermographic Survey · Flashing Repair

Structural Check
Structural check involves assessing the load-bearing adequacy of the roof structure — rafters, purlins, joists, ridge board, and wall plates — to confirm it can safely carry the existing or proposed roofing system. Structural checks are essential before re-roofing, loft conversion, or overlay installation. A structural engineer may specify temporary propping, sistering of rafters, or purlin reinforcement where original timbers are undersized or in poor condition. Metrotile's steel tiles, at 7–8 kg/m² installed weight, impose significantly less dead load than clay or concrete alternatives (typically 40–75 kg/m²), which often allows re-roofing without structural intervention.

Cleaning & Biological Growth

Preventive Maintenance
Preventive maintenance (also called proactive or pre-emptive maintenance) involves carrying out scheduled, routine care tasks to prevent deterioration and avoid reactive repairs. For a steel tile roof, preventive maintenance typically includes clearing gutters and valleys of debris, checking and re-sealing any flashing laps, inspecting ridge caps and dry-fix accessories, and treating any early-stage biological growth before it becomes established. A programme of preventive maintenance extends roof life, preserves the manufacturer's guarantee conditions, and significantly reduces the lifetime cost of ownership compared to a reactive-only approach.

See also: Planned Preventive Maintenance (PPM) · Annual Inspection

Annual Inspection
Annual inspection is a scheduled yearly check of a roof system, typically carried out each autumn to identify any issues before winter weather and to clear summer's accumulated debris. For Metrotile roofs, an annual inspection programme supports compliance with the 40-year guarantee conditions and provides an ongoing record of the roof's condition. In a social housing context, annual inspections are often written into asset management frameworks and planned preventive maintenance schedules. Most annual inspections of Metrotile roofs are brief and reassuring — the main checks are gutters, flashings, and ridge/hip accessories rather than the tiles themselves.

See also: Preventive Maintenance · Roof Inspection

Moss Removal
Moss removal is the process of eliminating moss growth from a roof surface to prevent it from lifting tiles, blocking gutters, and retaining moisture against the roof structure. Moss removal methods range from manual dry-scraping with a soft brush to chemical biocide treatment and specialist cleaning machines. For Metrotile's metallic ZAM®-coated tiles, moss establishment is far less common than on porous clay or concrete, but debris trapped in valleys or behind accessories can provide a growing medium. Any moss removal should avoid high-pressure washing that could damage the coating; soft-wash biocide methods are preferred.

See also: Biocide Wash · Algae Treatment

Algae Treatment
Algae treatment addresses the black, green, or orange discolouration caused by algae (particularly cyanobacteria such as Gloeocapsa magma) on roof surfaces. Algae thrives in damp, shaded conditions and is common on north-facing slopes. Treatment typically involves applying a low-concentration sodium hypochlorite or proprietary algaecide solution, which kills the organisms before they are washed away by rain. Preventive algaecide strips or treated tiles can provide long-term protection. Metrotile's coated steel surface is inherently less hospitable to algae than textured clay or concrete; nonetheless periodic treatment of heavily shaded roofs or those near mature trees is good practice.

Also called: Algaecide Treatment · Black Streak Removal

See also: Moss Removal · Biocide Wash

Biocide Wash
Biocide wash is the application of a chemical solution — typically containing quaternary ammonium compounds, sodium hypochlorite, or copper-based actives — to kill bacteria, algae, moss, lichens, and other biological growth on a roof. Biocide washes are applied by low-pressure spray and left to dwell; dead matter then degrades naturally and is washed away by rainfall over several weeks. They are safer for the tile coating than mechanical scraping or high-pressure jetting. Biocide products must comply with the UK Biocidal Products Regulation (BPR) and be used in accordance with the manufacturer's safety data sheet (SDS), including measures to protect gutters, drains, and vegetation from run-off.

Also called: Soft-Wash Treatment · Algaecide Spray

Pressure Washing
Pressure washing (power washing) uses high-pressure water jets to clean roof surfaces, typically at 1,200–2,500 psi. While effective at removing debris and surface growth quickly, high-pressure washing is generally not recommended for coated steel or colour-coated tile products, as it can damage or dislodge the protective coating, strip granule surfaces, and force water behind tiles or flashings. For Metrotile roofs, a soft-wash approach (low pressure, typically under 1,000 psi) combined with biocide treatment is preferred. Where pressure washing is deemed necessary, it should be carried out by a competent contractor who understands the coating's limitations and works downslope rather than against the tile profile.

See also: Biocide Wash · Moss Removal

Gutter Cleaning
Gutter cleaning is the removal of leaves, moss, silt, and debris from gutters and downpipes to maintain free-flowing rainwater drainage. Blocked gutters cause water to back up and overflow, potentially entering the eaves, saturating the fascia and soffit, and leading to damp penetration in the wall below. For most roofs, gutter cleaning is recommended at least twice a year — spring and autumn — and after any major storms. Gutter guard products (mesh or brush inserts) reduce the frequency required but do not eliminate the need entirely. Clean gutters are essential for preserving the warranty conditions of most roofing systems.

Also called: Gutter Clearance · Downpipe Clearing

Repair & Restoration

Touch-Up Paint
Touch-up paint is a colour-matched coating supplied by Metrotile for on-site application to cut tile edges, drilled holes, minor abrasions, or areas where the factory coating has been disturbed during installation or repair. Metrotile touch-up paint is formulated to match each tile colour and provides a corrosion-inhibiting barrier that restores the zinc-aluminium-magnesium alloy coating's protection at vulnerable cut or damaged edges. It is applied by brush to clean, dry metal. Touch-up paint is included in Metrotile's accessory range and should be used whenever tiles are cut or drilled on-site to maintain the full protection of the ZAM® coating and comply with guarantee conditions.

Tile Replacement
Tile replacement is the removal of a damaged, cracked, or missing tile and its substitution with a new tile of the same profile and colour. One of the significant advantages of Metrotile's interlocking panel system is that individual tiles can be replaced without disturbing the wider roof — the interlocking edges allow tiles to be lifted, slid free, and re-fixed with minimal disruption. The replacement tile is secured with the appropriate nail or screw fixing, re-interlocked with adjacent tiles, and any cut edges treated with touch-up paint. Where original tile colour has weathered, a sample comparison against current stock colour ranges should be made before replacement to ensure an acceptable match.

Ridge Repointing
Ridge repointing is the renewal of the mortar fillet along the underside of a traditional bedded ridge tile where mortar has cracked, loosened, or fallen away. On older roofs, ridge tiles were typically bedded in cement mortar; BS 5534:2014+A2:2018 now requires mechanical fixing as the primary fixing method for all ridges in new installations, with mortar used as a secondary cosmetic bedding only. For Metrotile roofs, ridge caps are installed as part of the dry-fix ridge system — there is no mortar to repoint. However, where Metrotile is installed on a property that retains traditional bedded ridges to other roof slopes, those sections may require periodic repointing.

Also called: Ridge Mortar Repair · Ridge Rebed

See also: Re-Bedding

Re-Bedding
Re-bedding is the replacement of deteriorated mortar bedding beneath ridge, hip, or verge tiles where the original bed has cracked, separated, or washed out. Traditional bedded ridges rely on a mortar fillet both to weather the joint and to contribute to fixing — when the mortar fails, ridges become loose, creating a safety hazard and allowing wind-driven rain to enter. Re-bedding involves cutting away old mortar, applying a fresh cement mortar or flexible pointing compound, and resetting the ridge tile with mechanical fixings per BS 5534. Metrotile's dry-fix accessory system eliminates the need for mortar bedding entirely, removing re-bedding from the maintenance schedule.

Also called: Ridge Rebed · Mortar Replacement

See also: Ridge Repointing

Flashing Repair
Flashing repair addresses deterioration or failure of the weatherproofing at roof junctions — typically abutment walls, chimney stacks, dormers, rooflights, and valleys — where flashings prevent water ingress at the interface between the roof covering and vertical or projecting elements. Common flashing failures include cracked or missing mortar pointing in stepped flashings, oxidised or split lead, delaminated self-adhesive flashings, and failed sealant beads. Repair may involve re-dressing and re-pointing existing lead, replacing a flashing section, re-bonding adhesive flashings, or applying compatible repair tape. For Metrotile roofs, compatibility with the ZAM® coating should be confirmed before applying any adhesive or sealant repair product.

See also: Flashings & Accessories Glossary

Sealant Renewal
Sealant renewal involves replacing aged, cracked, or failed silicone or polyurethane sealant at roof penetrations, flashing laps, pipe collars, and lead slate joints. Sealants have a finite service life — typically 10–20 years depending on product and UV exposure — after which they become brittle, crack, and lose adhesion, creating potential water ingress points. Sealant renewal should use products compatible with the adjacent materials (confirmed manufacturer guidance for lead, EPDM, aluminium, and coated steel respectively). All old sealant must be fully removed before the new bead is applied to a clean, dry surface. For Metrotile roofs, Metrotile's technical team can advise on compatible sealant products.

Also called: Silicone Replacement · Joint Re-Sealing

Guarantees & Lifespan

Product Lifespan
Product lifespan (also called service life) is the expected operational life of a roofing product under normal conditions of installation and maintenance. Metrotile's steel tiles, with their ZAM® zinc-aluminium-magnesium alloy coating, are specified to a 40-year design life supported by the manufacturer's guarantee and BBA Certificate 07/4470. By comparison, interlocking concrete tiles have a typical expected life of 60 years but are susceptible to delamination and colour fade; clay plain tiles can last 100+ years but are far heavier and more expensive. Whole-life cost analysis, which factors in replacement cycles, structural requirements, and maintenance costs, often favours lightweight steel over heavier alternatives even where upfront costs differ.

Manufacturer's Guarantee
Manufacturer's guarantee is a written commitment from the product manufacturer that the product will perform as specified for a defined period, with remedial action (repair, replacement, or refund) if it fails due to a manufacturing defect. Metrotile provides a 40-year product guarantee on its steel roofing tiles covering perforation from corrosion under normal UK conditions when installed by an approved contractor per the technical specification. The guarantee is registered at the time of installation and is transferable to subsequent owners of the property, adding meaningful value to the building's asset profile. Conditions include annual maintenance and avoidance of contact with dissimilar metals that could cause galvanic corrosion.

Also called: Product Guarantee · Manufacturer's Warranty

See also: Warranty

Warranty
Warranty in the roofing context refers to a contractual assurance — from either the product manufacturer or the installing contractor — that covers defects, failures, or non-performance for a specified period. Manufacturer's product warranties cover the tile or membrane itself; contractor workmanship warranties cover installation quality and typically run for 5–10 years from practical completion. Some clients require a single-point, collateral-backed warranty from a third-party insurer (such as Zurich or NHBC) that combines product and workmanship cover in one document and remains enforceable even if the contractor or manufacturer ceases trading.

See also: Manufacturer's Guarantee · Zurich Building Guarantee

Zurich Building Guarantee
Zurich Building Guarantee is an independently backed structural and weathertightness warranty provided by Zurich Insurance plc for new-build and conversion projects, including roofing installations. It is widely recognised by mortgage lenders and conveyancers as providing robust long-term protection for buyers. The guarantee covers structural defects for 10 years and, depending on scheme, weathertightness for the same period. Metrotile-approved contractors who are registered with Zurich can offer this guarantee as part of a re-roofing or new-build package, providing buyers and housing associations with confidence beyond the standard manufacturer's product guarantee.

Also called: Zurich New Build Guarantee

See also: Warranty

Planned Preventive Maintenance (PPM)
Planned preventive maintenance (PPM) is a formally scheduled programme of routine maintenance tasks carried out at defined intervals to preserve asset condition, comply with guarantee requirements, and prevent costly reactive repairs. For roofing, a PPM schedule for a housing stock or commercial estate would typically include annual gutter cleaning, biennial roof inspections, five-yearly flashing checks, and decennial condition surveys. PPM is a core component of asset management plans required by local authorities, registered social landlords, and property companies under their long-term stock condition strategies. Metrotile's low-maintenance steel tile system significantly simplifies PPM programmes compared to clay or concrete tile roofs, reducing scheduled intervention frequency and cost.
