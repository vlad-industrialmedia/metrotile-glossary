Caps & Trims

Ridge Cap
A ridge cap is the finishing trim that covers the apex of a pitched roof where two roof slopes meet at the ridge line. On a Metrotile system, the ridge cap is a purpose-made roll-formed steel component, colour-matched and coated to match the tiles, which mechanically clips or screws to the ridge board or ridge batten. The ridge cap must allow the thermal movement of the steel tile plane while maintaining a weatherproof seal; internally, an eaves-to-ridge ventilation path under the tile field can exit beneath or through the ridge cap to ventilate a cold roof void. Under BS 5534:2014+A2:2018, all ridge fixings must be mechanically fixed — mortar-bedded ridges alone are no longer compliant.

See also: Dry-Fix Ridge · Hip Cap

Hip Cap
A hip cap is the weatherproof trim that covers a hip rafter — the sloping line where two adjoining roof slopes meet at an external angle. Like the ridge cap, Metrotile hip caps are roll-formed steel, colour-matched, and mechanically fixed rather than bedded in mortar. Hip caps must accommodate the geometry of the hip angle (typically 90–135° plan angle) and the change in tile cutting at each course as the hip converges toward the ridge. At the junction of the hip and ridge, a specially formed hip-to-ridge junction piece completes the weathering. Dry-fix mechanical hip fixings are specified in accordance with BS 5534.

See also: Ridge Cap

Ridge Roll & Dry-Fix Ridge
A ridge roll is a flexible polyurethane or EPDM foam strip that seals the gap between the underside of the ridge cap and the top course of tiles, preventing bird, insect, and wind-blown debris ingress without impeding ventilation. The ridge roll compresses to conform to the tile profile. A dry-fix ridge system is the BS 5534-compliant mechanical alternative to mortar bedding at ridges and hips: it uses a combination of batten clips, mechanical screws, and foam closures to secure the ridge cap without mortar. Metrotile's ridge system is fully dry-fix, eliminating the cracking, spalling, and frost damage that afflicts mortared ridges, and avoiding the scaffold return visits for remedial repointing common on traditional clay and concrete tile roofs.

Synonyms: Foam filler · Eaves filler foam

See also: Ridge Cap

Eaves Filler Strip
An eaves filler strip (also called a bottom course filler or starter filler) is a profiled foam or pre-formed steel strip installed at the eaves immediately beneath the first (bottom) course of tiles. It fills the open voids in the tile profile at eaves level, preventing birds and vermin from nesting in the roof space while maintaining a controlled ventilation gap. On Metrotile systems, the eaves filler strip is profile-specific — shaped to match the geometry of each tile pattern — and is installed before the starter course is laid. It is distinct from the over-fascia vent, which provides the primary air intake into the eaves void.

Synonyms: Bottom course filler · Starter filler

See also: Over-Fascia Vent

Barge Board & Verge Trim
A barge board is the board (traditionally timber, now often uPVC or aluminium) fixed to the outer face of the gable end rafter or verge, finishing the roof edge and protecting the exposed ends of the roof structure from weather. In a dry-verge system, the barge board is supplemented or replaced by a verge trim — a mechanically fixed plastic or aluminium capping that clips over the last tile course at the verge, eliminating the need for mortar bedding and pointing. Metrotile's dry-verge trim system is designed to interlock with the tile profile at the gable edge, providing a weathertight, maintenance-free finish that complies with BS 5534's mechanical fixing requirements.

Synonyms: Verge cap · Dry verge · Gable trim

Valleys & Flashings

Valley Lining
Valley lining is the material used to form the waterproof channel at the intersection of two roof slopes (the valley). Valley linings collect and direct rainwater from both slopes into the gutter. On Metrotile roofs, valley linings are typically formed from either a GRP (glass-reinforced polyester) moulded valley unit or a lead/lead-free flashing dressed into a valley channel formed by the underlay and tilting fillet. The valley width must accommodate the maximum calculated run-off from both slopes without overtopping; BS EN 12056-3 provides sizing guidance. All valleys should be underlaid continuously with an additional strip of underlay lapped beneath the main underlay.

See also: GRP Valley · Lead Flashing

GRP Valley
A GRP valley is a factory-moulded glass-reinforced polyester (fibreglass) valley liner that provides a pre-formed, dimensionally stable, and waterproof channel for Metrotile installations. GRP valleys are lighter and easier to handle than lead, require no dressing or forming on site, and are available in lengths to suit standard rafter spacings. They are UV-stabilised and colour-matched (typically dark brown or black) for aesthetic discretion. GRP valleys are the preferred valley solution for Metrotile in most applications; they are compatible with any pitch from 10° upwards and eliminate the skill-dependency of lead dressing. They should be installed over a continuous strip of underlay and supported on tilting fillets or valley boards.

Synonyms: Fibreglass valley · Moulded valley

See also: Valley Lining

Flashing
Flashing is a thin impermeable material — typically lead, lead-free sheet metal, or flexible polymer — used to weatherproof junctions between a roof covering and adjacent vertical elements (walls, chimneys, dormers, pipe penetrations). Flashings direct water away from potentially vulnerable junctions and are critical to maintaining roof weathertightness. They are classified by position: apron (front), step (side), back gutter, and soaker. On Metrotile roofs, flashings are most commonly required at abutment walls, around chimney stacks, at dormer cheeks, and around pipe penetrations. The flashing material must be chemically compatible with the tile coating and the substrate to which it is fixed.

See also: Lead Flashing · Lead-Free Flashing

Lead Flashing
Lead flashing uses milled lead sheet (to BS EN 12588) to form the weather seal at roof junctions. Lead is the traditional UK flashing material: it is ductile (easy to dress into complex shapes), long-lived (80–100 year service life), self-healing for minor gaps, and universally accepted by planners including in conservation areas and for listed buildings. Metrotile steel tiles are fully compatible with lead flashing; there is no galvanic corrosion risk between the ZAM® coating and lead in normal exposure conditions. Lead sheet is classified by code (1–8), with Code 3 (1.32 mm, 14.97 kg/m²) used for soakers and Code 4 (1.80 mm, 20.41 kg/m²) for flashings, in accordance with the Lead Sheet Association's manual and BS 6915.

See also: Lead-Free Flashing · Flashing

Lead-Free Flashing
Lead-free flashing products are purpose-designed alternatives to traditional lead sheet, used where lead is restricted — near drinking water catchments, in schools and children's settings, in areas sensitive to lead contamination, or simply where a lighter and easier-to-handle material is preferred. Common lead-free flashing products include: Wakaflex (bitumen-polymer strip with woven aluminium mesh), Ubiflex (multi-layer elastomeric strip), Sandtoft Lead-Free (aluminium-core polymer composite), and aluminium step flashing systems. All are compatible with Metrotile steel tiles and ZAM® coatings; compatibility with specific sealants and fixing methods should be verified for each product and application.

Synonyms: Flexible flashing · Lead alternative

See also: Lead Flashing

Lead Slate & Vent Tile
A lead slate (or pipe flashing) is a pre-formed lead or rubber collar that fits around a circular pipe penetration through the roof — typically a soil vent pipe (SVP), boiler flue, or mechanical ventilation duct. The lead slate dresses over the tile surface and is sealed at the pipe with a stainless-steel jubilee clip. A vent tile is a purpose-made tile with a circular or rectangular aperture through which a pipe or duct passes, providing a weatherproof penetration without cutting tiles on site. Metrotile supplies profile-specific vent tiles for SVPs and mechanical ventilation outlets, designed to match the tile profile and provide a weather seal without mortar.

Synonyms: Pipe flashing · Soil pipe flashing

Underlay & Battens

Breathable Underlay
Breathable underlay (vapour-permeable underlay, VPU) is a woven or non-woven polypropylene or polyethylene membrane that functions as the secondary weather barrier beneath the tile field while permitting water vapour diffusion from the roof space to the external environment. Breathable underlays for pitched roofs must meet the high-resistance (HR) classification of BS EN 13859-1 and BS 4016 — meaning they restrict water infiltration while maintaining adequate vapour permeance. Their use with Metrotile eliminates the need for eaves-level and ridge-level ventilation gaps in most cold-roof assemblies, simplifying construction and improving airtightness. Laps must be a minimum 150 mm at horizontal joints and 100 mm at vertical joints, fixed in accordance with BS 5534.

Synonyms: Vapour-permeable underlay · VPU · Breather membrane

See also: VPU · Felt Lap

Vapour-Permeable Underlay (VPU)
Vapour-permeable underlay (VPU) is the technical designation for the class of breathable roofing membranes that combines water resistance (Sd value ≥ 0.3 m or equivalent) with vapour permeability (water vapour transmission rate meeting HR classification). VPUs are required when BS 5250 ventilation calculation shows that the roof space will not maintain acceptable humidity without a vapour-permeable secondary layer — which is the case for most modern cold-roof assemblies. VPUs should not be confused with vapour control layers (VCLs), which are installed on the warm side of insulation to restrict vapour flow into the roof structure; VPUs are installed on the cold side, above the insulation, to allow vapour to escape.

See also: Breathable Underlay

Felt Lap
A felt lap is the overlap between adjacent sheets of roofing underlay — the distance by which one sheet of membrane overlaps the one below or beside it. Minimum lap dimensions are specified in BS 5534 and the underlay manufacturer's installation instructions; typical minimum horizontal (head) laps are 150 mm and vertical (side) laps are 100 mm. Laps must be oriented so that the upper sheet overlaps the lower (shingle fashion) to shed any water that penetrates the tile field. At rafter ends and eaves, the underlay should be dressed into the gutter by at least 50 mm to direct any water clear of the fascia and into the drainage system.

See also: Breathable Underlay

Counter-Batten
A counter-batten (or vertical batten) is a batten fixed directly to the rafter over the underlay, running up the slope, onto which the horizontal tile battens are then nailed. Counter-battens serve two purposes: they create a ventilated void between the underlay and the tile battens (the 'batten space'), allowing air to circulate and any water that penetrates the tile lap to drain to the eaves without ponding on the membrane; and they provide a means of nailing the tile battens without compressing the underlay excessively. Counter-battens are required when using a vapour-permeable underlay in a cold-roof assembly and are specified in BS 5534. Metrotile installation guides specify the required counter-batten dimension for each product and pitch.

Synonyms: Vertical batten · Rafter batten

See also: Tile Batten

Tile Batten
Tile battens (also called roofing battens or horizontal battens) are pressure-treated softwood sections fixed horizontally across the rafters (or counter-battens) at regular intervals (the 'gauge') to provide the fixing substrate for the tiles. Batten spacing is determined by the tile gauge — the distance between courses — which for Metrotile is fixed by the tile geometry (typically 300–400 mm depending on profile). Batten sizing must be determined by structural design in accordance with BS 5534 and BS 8103-3 to span between rafters at the specified spacing under tile, wind uplift, and maintenance loads. Standard sizes are 25×50 mm (short rafter spans) to 38×50 mm or 50×50 mm (longer spans). All battens must be BS 8417-treated to Use Class UC2 for above-ground protected conditions.

Synonyms: Roofing batten · Horizontal batten

See also: Counter-Batten

Over-Fascia Vent
An over-fascia vent is a perforated or meshed strip fixed over the top of the fascia board, creating a controlled ventilation gap between the fascia and the bottom edge of the underlay. It provides the primary air inlet for eaves-to-ridge cross-ventilation in a cold-roof assembly, while excluding birds, insects, and wind-blown debris. Minimum free ventilation area required by BS 5250 at eaves is 25 mm equivalent free area (EFA) for cold roofs with a horizontal ceiling. Over-fascia vents are available in uPVC or aluminium in widths to suit standard fascia depths and tile eaves depths. On a breathable membrane roof, over-fascia vents are still recommended at eaves to introduce air into the batten space and prevent dead zones of humid air.

Synonyms: Eaves vent · Fascia vent

Fixings & Tools

Clout Nail
Clout nails are short, large-headed galvanised steel nails used to fix roofing underlay and sometimes tile battens to rafters. The large flat head ('clout') distributes the load over the membrane, preventing the nail from pulling through during wind uplift. For underlay fixing, clout nails are typically 20–25 mm × 3.35 mm shank, hot-dip galvanised to BS 1202. Where clout nails are used for tile fixing (on some systems), they must be specified in accordance with BS 5534 wind uplift calculations and be of the correct length and diameter to achieve the required characteristic withdrawal resistance in the batten timber. On Metrotile systems, tiles are typically fixed with clout nails or annular ring shank nails through the pre-punched tile holes.

See also: Annular Ring Shank Nail · Batten Screw

Annular Ring Shank Nail
Annular ring shank nails (also called ring shank nails or helical shank nails) are nails with a series of annular or helical grooves around the shank that greatly increase withdrawal resistance compared to smooth shank nails of the same diameter. They are the preferred tile fixing nail in high wind zones because their enhanced grip in the batten timber significantly reduces the risk of tile uplift. BS 5534 specifies that fixings for tiles in Exposure Zones 2, 3, and 4, and at exposed eaves and verge positions, must be of a type that achieves the required design uplift resistance — which for timber battens typically means ring shank or screw shank nails rather than plain smooth nails. All tile fixing nails for Metrotile should be hot-dip galvanised or stainless steel.

Synonyms: Ring shank nail · Helical nail

See also: Clout Nail

Batten Screw
Batten screws are structural screws used to fix tile battens to rafters (or counter-battens to rafters), providing higher withdrawal resistance than nails in engineered timber and modified wood species, and in wet or damp conditions where nails may corrode and lose grip. Under BS 5534 and BS 8103-3, batten fixings in Exposure Zone 3 or 4, or where rafter timber is green or of uncertain moisture content, should be screws rather than nails. Self-drilling or self-tapping stainless-steel or galvanised screws of the specified diameter and length must achieve the minimum characteristic withdrawal resistance required by the fixing design for the tile's uplift load. Screw-fixed battens also facilitate easier removal and replacement during re-roofing.

See also: Tile Batten

Tile Clip
Tile clips are stainless-steel or galvanised wire or pressed steel clips that mechanically restrain individual tiles to the batten at positions where the nail fixing alone would be insufficient to resist BS 5534 uplift forces — typically at eaves, verge, ridge courses, and in high-wind-zone roofs where every tile must be clipped. For Metrotile's interlocking steel tile system, the mechanical interlock between tiles provides significant inherent wind resistance, but additional tile clips may be specified at exposed positions on the basis of a BS 5534 fixing design calculation. Clips add minimal cost but can significantly improve performance in severe weather.

See also: Ring Shank Nail

Touch-Up Paint
Touch-up paint is a colour-matched coating supplied by Metrotile for treating cut edges, small surface scratches, and minor site-handling damage to the stone chip or PVDF coating on steel tiles. Steel tile cut edges — produced when tiles are cut to fit at valleys, hips, and abutments — expose the bare steel substrate, which must be coated within the working day to prevent corrosion, particularly in coastal or aggressive environments. Metrotile touch-up paint is supplied in colour-matched aerosol or brush-apply format and should be applied to all cut edges in accordance with the BBA Certificate conditions. Where tiles are cut with an angle grinder (not recommended), edge treatment with touch-up paint is mandatory.

Synonyms: Edge sealer · Cut edge treatment

Tile Guillotine
A tile guillotine is the recommended cutting tool for Metrotile steel roofing tiles. The hand-operated or powered guillotine produces a clean straight cut through the steel substrate and stone chip surface without generating the heat and sparks of angle grinders, which damage the ZAM® coating and stone chip. Guillotines available for Metrotile use include purpose-designed bench-top models used on site, and hand-held tin snips for small adjustments. Straight cuts for valley tile trimming, verge cuts, and cutting tiles to length around obstructions can all be achieved with the guillotine. For circular penetrations (pipe flashings), a purpose-made hole saw is used rather than the guillotine.

Synonyms: Metal tile cutter

See also: Touch-Up Paint

Rainwater & Ancillary

Fascia & Soffit
The fascia is the board fixed vertically to the ends of the roof rafters at eaves level, forming the front face of the eaves. The soffit is the horizontal board (or panel) closing the underside of the eaves between the fascia and the wall. Together, fascia and soffit enclose the eaves void, protect the rafter ends from weather, and provide the mounting surface for gutters (fascia) and ventilation (soffit). On Metrotile roofs, the fascia depth must accommodate the gutter bracket fixing and be sufficient to support the over-fascia vent. uPVC fascia and soffit are most common in new build and refurbishment; timber sections (painted or treated hardwood) are used in heritage applications.

See also: Gutter · Over-Fascia Vent

Gutter & Downpipe
Gutters and downpipes form the rainwater drainage system that collects run-off from the roof surface and directs it to the drainage network. Half-round and square-section gutters in uPVC, aluminium, or cast iron are the most common residential types. Gutter sizing must be based on the calculated run-off from the roof plan catchment area, accounting for the local rainfall intensity (typically the 2-minute 1-in-2-year rate from the UK Rainfall Atlas) and the roof pitch — steeper pitches direct water into the gutter faster, effectively increasing the design flow rate. BS EN 12056-3 (gravity drainage systems inside buildings) and BS EN 612 (gutters and downpipes for discontinuous roofing) provide sizing methodology. Metrotile installation guidance gives eaves details to ensure run-off enters the gutter cleanly.

See also: Fascia

Roof Light & Skylight
Roof lights (skylights) are glazed openings in the roof slope that admit daylight and can provide ventilation. Types include fixed flat-glass roof lights, openable centre-pivot or top-hung windows (Velux, Fakro), and bespoke structural glazing. Integrating a roof light into a Metrotile steel tile roof requires a correctly formed kerb, upstand flashing, and dressed lead or lead-free flashing around the perimeter. The roof light manufacturer will specify the minimum pitch and the required flashing kit for their product; Metrotile tiles are trimmed to the surrounding coursing, with the gap covered by the flashing. Where cutting is required, all cut edges are treated with touch-up paint. Part L and Part F may require minimum window-to-floor-area ratios and opening lights in habitable rooms, influencing roof light specification.

Synonyms: Skylight · Velux window

See also: Lead Flashing

Snow Guard
Snow guards are devices fixed to the roof surface or battens to retain accumulated snow on the roof and prevent it from sliding off in large sheets — which can injure people below, damage gutters, and block entrances. They are particularly important on low-friction smooth surfaces (metal roofing, slate), on roofs over public footpaths, doorways, and car parking areas, and in high-altitude or high-snowfall locations. For Metrotile steel tile roofs in the UK, snow guards are not routinely required for typical residential applications in lowland areas, but should be considered for roofs in Highland Scotland, upland Wales and northern England, and in public-access commercial contexts. Snow guard selection and fixing must account for the snow load per BS EN 1991-1-3 and EN 1298.

Synonyms: Snow retention system
