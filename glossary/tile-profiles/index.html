Core Metrotile Profiles

Metrotile Shingle
Metrotile Shingle is a steel roofing tile with a flat, slightly textured surface profile that replicates the appearance of traditional cedar wood shingles or North American asphalt shingles. The profile features a horizontal shadow line at each tile course, creating depth and character on the finished roof. Each tile panel covers approximately 0.55 m² and weighs around 4.5 kg — giving an installed weight of approximately 7–8 kg/m² including fixings and underlay. The Shingle is suitable from a minimum pitch of 10° and is one of Metrotile's bestselling profiles for residential re-roofing, new build and conservatory solid roof projects.

See also: Stone chip coating · ZAM® coating · Dry-fix system

Metrotile Bond
Metrotile Bond is a flat, interlocking steel tile profile designed to replicate the appearance of traditional concrete interlocking or clay plain tiles on a pitched roof. The Bond's low-profile, wide-coverage format suits both contemporary and traditional UK roof designs. Each panel covers a generous area, reducing the number of tiles and fixings required per m² compared to natural materials. Bond is widely used in social housing refurbishment and new build developments where a clean, uniform aesthetic is required alongside BBA-certified performance.

See also: Flat tile · BBA Certificate

→ Metrotile Bond product page

Metrotile Roman
Metrotile Roman is a steel tile profile with a curved, barrel-style cross-section that replicates the look of traditional Italian or Spanish clay Roman tiles. The raised barrel rib creates a strong three-dimensional profile visible from ground level, making Roman popular for south-facing elevations, country houses and properties where a Mediterranean aesthetic is desired. The profile's curvature also assists rainwater run-off, contributing to effective drainage performance at pitches as low as 15°. Metrotile Roman is BBA certified and available in a full range of stone chip colours.

Synonyms: curved tile · barrel tile profile · pantile-style

→ Metrotile Roman product page

Metrotile Woodshake
Metrotile Woodshake is a steel roofing tile pressed with a deeply embossed, split-wood grain surface texture that faithfully replicates the appearance of hand-split cedar or pine wood shakes. The three-dimensional surface texture creates authentic shadow lines and grain variation across the roof, giving a natural wood appearance without the fire risk, maintenance requirements or environmental impact of real timber. Woodshake is popular for log cabins, country houses, lodges and properties requiring a natural rural aesthetic. Minimum pitch: 15°.

Synonyms: wood shake tile · shake-profile tile

→ Metrotile Woodshake product page

Metrotile Qube
Metrotile Qube is a contemporary, large-format flat steel tile profile with a clean, square-edged aesthetic suited to modern architecture, self-build homes and commercial buildings. The Qube's minimal profile creates a sleek, low-relief roofscape that complements flat-pack, modular and Scandinavian-influenced architectural styles. Its large coverage area reduces the number of panels required per m² and minimises lap joints, contributing to a very clean finished appearance. The Qube is particularly well-suited to steeper pitches and prominent roof planes visible from above.

See also: Flat tile · Modular home

Metrotile Viksen
Metrotile Viksen is a Scandinavian-inspired steel roofing tile featuring a distinctive diagonal or chevron-pattern emboss on the tile surface. The Viksen profile creates a characterful, textured roofscape that stands apart from conventional plain or shingle profiles, and it is popular on residential properties, lodges, chalets and holiday homes in upland or coastal locations. The diagonal pattern helps shed water effectively even at lower pitches, and the profile is available in Metrotile's standard stone chip colour range.

See also: Low-pitch roof

Metrotile iPanel Slate
Metrotile iPanel Slate is a large-format steel panel tile with a riven, slate-textured surface that replicates the appearance of natural Welsh or Cumbrian slate. The iPanel format covers a substantial roof area per panel, reducing installation time and labour cost compared to small-format natural or fibre-cement slates. The product is particularly popular for re-roofing projects where speed of installation is a priority — such as social housing programmes, schools and healthcare facilities — while the slate-like aesthetic preserves design intent. Minimum pitch: 12°.

Synonyms: slate-profile steel tile · large-format slate tile

→ Metrotile iPanel Slate product page

Metrotile Aquapan
Metrotile Aquapan is a specialist low-pitch steel roofing tile engineered for installation at pitches as low as 10° — and in certain conditions down to 5° with additional underlay precautions. The Aquapan profile uses an enhanced interlocking geometry and longer tile overlap to maintain a weathertight seal at very shallow roof angles where conventional tiles would be vulnerable to wind-driven rain ingress. Aquapan is commonly used for garage roofs, flat-to-pitch conversions, dormer cheeks, extensions and agricultural buildings where the existing structure constrains the achievable pitch.

See also: Flat-to-pitch conversion · Minimum pitch

Cambridge Xtreme 9.5 Shingle
Cambridge Xtreme 9.5 Shingle (also written Cambridge Xtreme or Cambridge 9.5) is a heavy-duty steel roofing tile profile engineered to withstand extreme weather conditions including high-wind zones, coastal environments and areas prone to ice and hail. The "9.5" designation refers to the tile's gauge specification, which uses heavier steel than the standard range to provide additional rigidity and impact resistance. Cambridge Xtreme combines a shingle-style aesthetic with enhanced structural performance, making it a popular choice for exposed upland sites, Scottish highlands and coastal projects. Minimum pitch: 10°.

Synonyms: Cambridge Xtreme · Cambridge 9.5

Decra Range

Decra Classic
Decra Classic is a premium steel roofing tile in the Decra range, featuring a flat pantile-inspired profile with a twice-baked acrylic stone chip coating on a ZINCALUME® steel substrate. The twice-baked coating process ensures superior colour depth, UV stability and chip adhesion compared to standard stone chip tiles. Decra Classic suits traditional architectural styles and heritage re-roofing projects, and is available in a curated range of muted, period-appropriate colours. The product carries the Metrotile 40-year guarantee and is compatible with the full range of Metrotile accessories.

Decra Oberon
Decra Oberon is a bold, deeply-profiled interlocking steel shingle tile in the Decra range. The Oberon's pronounced shadow lines and robust surface texture create a striking visual effect on steep-pitch roofs, making it popular for prominent residential properties, lodges and farmhouses. Like all Decra products, the Oberon uses ZINCALUME® steel and twice-baked stone chip coating, providing exceptional weather resistance and colour retention in coastal and upland exposures. The profile's interlocking design ensures wind resistance performance far exceeding that of most traditional materials.

Decra Stratos L
Decra Stratos L is a large-format, slate-look steel tile in the Decra premium range, designed to replicate the appearance of natural thin slate whilst delivering the weight, durability and installation speed advantages of steel. The "L" designation refers to the large panel format, which covers a greater area per tile than standard slate-look profiles. Decra Stratos L is favoured for contemporary and luxury residential projects, barn conversions and properties in conservation areas where a slate appearance is required but natural slate is impractical due to weight or availability constraints.

Specialist & Niche Profiles

eQube Solar Tile
Metrotile eQube Solar Tile is a building-integrated photovoltaic (BIPV) roofing panel that incorporates monocrystalline silicon solar cells directly into a steel roofing tile, eliminating the need for separate rack-mounted solar modules. The eQube uses the same interlocking fixing system as standard Metrotile Qube panels, so the solar tiles integrate seamlessly into a conventional steel tile roof installation. Each eQube panel generates approximately 20W of electrical output under standard test conditions (STC), and multiple panels are wired in series/parallel to feed a string inverter. The system preserves the roof's clean aesthetic whilst generating renewable electricity from the building envelope.

See also: BIPV · Solar tile · MCS

→ eQube Solar Tile product page

Slate 900 Anti-Vandal
Metrotile Slate 900 Anti-Vandal is a reinforced steel roofing tile system specifically engineered for high-security or vulnerable-premises applications — such as schools, social housing, community buildings and commercial properties where standard roof tiles may be subject to deliberate damage or theft. The Slate 900 features a heavier-gauge steel substrate and a secure mechanical fixing system designed to resist forced removal. The slate-textured surface provides an architecturally appropriate appearance whilst the enhanced specification deters opportunistic tile theft and vandalism.

See also: Schools · Social housing

Profile Terminology

Interlocking tile
An interlocking tile is a roofing tile designed so that its edges slot together with adjacent tiles, forming a mechanical joint that resists wind-driven rain ingress without the need for mortar. Most Metrotile profiles use an interlocking or lapping system, enabling rapid installation and delivering weather resistance that meets or exceeds the requirements of BS 5534 (British Standard for slating and tiling). Interlocking tiles allow a dry-fix installation, avoiding the cracking and maintenance issues associated with mortar bedding.

See also: Dry-fix system · BS 5534

Modular tile
A modular tile is a roofing tile manufactured to a consistent, standardised panel size so that tiles from the same profile range can be combined with accessories (ridge, hip, verge, valley) in a precisely coordinated system. All Metrotile profiles are modular: the tile dimensions, batten spacing, ridge height and accessory geometry are engineered to work together. Modular coordination simplifies estimating, ordering and installation, reducing waste and ensuring a consistent roofline.
Steel roofing tile
A steel roofing tile is a roofing panel manufactured from cold-rolled galvanised steel, press-formed to replicate the profile of traditional roofing materials (slate, clay, concrete, timber). Steel tiles are typically 70–80% lighter than equivalent concrete or natural slate tiles, enabling re-roofing without structural reinforcement and installation at lower minimum pitches. Metrotile steel tiles carry a metallic coating (ZAM® or ZINCALUME®) for corrosion resistance, plus a factory-bonded stone chip or acrylic finish for aesthetics and additional protection.

See also: Cold-rolled steel · ZAM® coating

Stone chip coating
Stone chip coating is the protective and decorative surface layer applied to Metrotile steel tiles, consisting of crushed mineral aggregate (granite, quartzite or ceramic granules) bonded to the steel substrate with an acrylic adhesive under high pressure. The stone chip layer provides UV protection, weather resistance, enhanced grip for foot traffic during installation, and a tactile, non-reflective appearance that closely resembles natural roofing materials. Colour stability is maintained over decades; Decra products use a twice-baked acrylic process for enhanced performance.

Synonyms: stone chip finish · granule coating · aggregate coating

Tile profile
The tile profile describes the cross-sectional shape of a roofing tile as seen from the end — defining whether it is flat, curved, ribbed, corrugated or embossed. Profile choice affects the roof's visual appearance, drainage performance and installation details. Metrotile offers profiles ranging from completely flat (Qube) to deeply curved (Roman) and textured/embossed (Woodshake, Viksen). The profile also influences the required batten spacing and the design of complementary accessories such as ridge and hip caps.
Heritage profile
A heritage profile is a tile design specifically intended to replicate the aesthetic of traditional historic roofing materials — most commonly hand-made clay plain tiles, natural slate, clay pantiles or cedar wood shakes — for use on period properties, conservation area buildings and listed building settings. Metrotile's Woodshake, Roman and iPanel Slate profiles are frequently specified as heritage profiles where planning requirements mandate a traditional material appearance but structural, weight or maintenance considerations make natural materials impractical.

See also: Conservation area · Listed building

Pan tile
A pan tile (also pantile) is a S-shaped or N-shaped interlocking clay or concrete tile with a distinctive undulating profile that provides a Mediterranean or vernacular appearance. Metrotile does not manufacture a traditional clay pan tile, but the Roman and Decra Classic profiles are frequently used as steel alternatives where a pantile-style appearance is desired — offering equivalent visual character at a fraction of the weight and with superior durability and warranty coverage.
Flat tile
A flat tile is a roofing tile with a planar or near-planar top surface, as opposed to profiled tiles with curves, ribs or embossing. Metrotile's Qube, Bond and iPanel Slate are the closest to flat-tile profiles in the range. Flat tiles typically require a steeper minimum pitch than profiled tiles, as the absence of profile ribs means the tile relies more heavily on tile-to-tile overlap for weathertightness. Flat tiles are popular in contemporary architecture and minimalist roof designs.
Abutment tile
An abutment tile (or abutment piece) is a specially shaped tile or metal flashing component used where a roof surface meets a vertical wall, chimney breast, parapet or gable wall. At an abutment, the standard tile profile must terminate cleanly against the vertical surface, with a step flashing or cover flashing sealing the junction to prevent water ingress. Metrotile supplies abutment pieces matched to each profile and provides fixing and detailing guidance in its installation guide.

See also: Step flashing · Abutment

Hip tile
A hip tile is a purpose-made roofing component that covers and weatherproofs the hip — the external angle formed where two roof planes meet at a sloping ridge. On a Metrotile installation, the hip is finished using a profiled metal hip cap system that interlocks with the tile courses below, maintaining a consistent aesthetic with the main roof surface. Hip tiles must be fixed securely against wind uplift and are designed to manage rainwater run-off from both adjacent roof slopes.

See also: Ridge tile · Hip finish · Hip

Ridge tile
A ridge tile is a purpose-made roofing component that caps and seals the apex of a pitched roof — the ridge — where the two main roof planes meet. Metrotile ridge caps are profiled steel components designed to match the visual character of each tile range. They interlock with the topmost tile courses and are secured with screws to the ridge board. The Metrotile dry-fix ridge system avoids mortar, which can crack and require periodic repointing.

See also: Ridge cap · Ridge

Verge tile
A verge tile is a tile or trim piece that finishes the exposed edge of a pitched roof at a gable wall — the verge. Standard field tiles must be cut to the verge line and covered with a profiled verge trim to prevent wind-driven rain penetrating beneath the tile edge. Metrotile verge trims are pressed-steel components designed to match the tile profile and fix mechanically to the bargeboard or gable wall, providing a clean, maintenance-free finish.

See also: Verge detail · Verge

Valley tile
A valley tile is a purpose-made component installed in the valley — the internal angle between two intersecting roof planes — to manage rainwater flowing from both slopes. Metrotile valley linings are GRP (glass-reinforced polyester) moulded valley channels, factory-formed to channel water to the eaves. The valley lining sits beneath the tile courses on both sides of the valley and is designed with a generous width to handle high rainfall flow rates on large roof catchments.

See also: GRP valley lining · Valley

Colour range
Metrotile's colour range refers to the selection of stone chip colours available across the product range. Standard colours include Charcoal (dark grey, the most popular), Graphite, Brown, Terracotta, Rustic Red, Green and various project-specific shades. Colour is integral to the stone chip coating and does not fade or peel in the same way as painted finishes — the aggregate granules retain their pigmentation over the product's 40-year design life. Colour availability may vary between profile ranges; the full selection is shown in Metrotile's product brochure and sample swatch kit.
