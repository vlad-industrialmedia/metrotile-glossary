Planning

Planning Permission
Planning permission is the formal consent granted by the Local Planning Authority (LPA) that authorises development — including material changes to the external appearance of a building. In England, planning is governed by the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended) and the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). For most residential re-roofing projects, planning permission is not required because the work constitutes Permitted Development. However, changing the roof material to something substantially different in appearance, replacing a roof in a conservation area, or carrying out any works to a listed building will require planning permission and/or Listed Building Consent. Applications are made via the LPA's planning portal (Planning Portal England) and typically take 8 weeks for a householder application.

See also: Permitted Development · Listed Building Consent

Permitted Development
Permitted Development (PD) rights are a national grant of planning permission for certain types of development, established in the Town and Country Planning (General Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015 (GPDO), which allows specified works to be carried out without making a formal planning application. For dwellinghouses, the relevant permitted development class for re-roofing is Class C (Other alterations to the roof of a dwellinghouse): alterations that do not materially affect the external appearance of the building do not require a planning application. Replacing old concrete tiles with Metrotile steel tiles of a similar profile and colour is generally considered PD. PD rights are removed for listed buildings, can be removed by LPA Article 4 Directions in conservation areas, and differ in Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland.

See also: Planning Permission · Conservation Area

Listed Building Consent
Listed Building Consent (LBC) is a separate statutory consent, in addition to planning permission, required for any works that would affect the character of a listed building in England — including re-roofing, alterations to the roof structure, adding rooflights, or changing flashing materials. It is granted by the LPA and applications are assessed against the duty to preserve the building or its setting under the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990. Unauthorised works to a listed building are a criminal offence. Where Metrotile tiles are proposed for a listed building, a pre-application discussion with the LPA conservation officer is essential, and submission of profile samples, U-value calculations, and evidence of use on comparable buildings will support the application.

See also: Planning Permission · Heritage Building

Pre-Application Advice
Pre-application advice (pre-app) is an informal, paid consultation with the LPA's planning officers before submitting a formal planning application. It allows the applicant to test the acceptability of a proposal, understand the LPA's likely concerns, and refine the scheme to maximise its chances of approval — reducing the risk of refusal and appeal. For roofing projects in sensitive locations (conservation areas, AONBs, settings of listed buildings), pre-application dialogue with the LPA's planning officer or conservation officer is strongly recommended before specifying a product or preparing detailed drawings. Metrotile's technical team can provide samples, profile data sheets, and precedent evidence packages to support a pre-application approach.

Synonyms: Pre-app · Pre-application consultation

Building Regulations
The Building Regulations (Building Regulations 2010, as amended) set minimum standards for the design, construction, and alteration of buildings in England and Wales, covering structural stability, fire safety, energy efficiency, ventilation, drainage, and accessibility. For roofing, the relevant Parts are: Part A (Structure — roof loads, batten sizing, rafter design); Part B (Fire safety — fire classification of cladding materials); Part C (Resistance to contaminants and moisture — underlay, eaves ventilation); Part L (Conservation of fuel and power — U-value, airtightness); and Part F (Ventilation — roof space ventilation). Building Regulations compliance is assessed and approved by the local Building Control Body (BCB) or an Approved Inspector, who will inspect the works and issue a completion certificate.

See also: Building Control

Building Control & Approved Inspector
Building control is the statutory process for verifying that building work complies with the Building Regulations. In England, this can be carried out by the local authority's Building Control department or by a private Approved Inspector (now called Registered Building Control Approver under the Building Safety Act 2022). For notifiable roofing work (new dwellings, extensions, flat-to-pitch conversions, and re-roofings where Building Regulations apply), a Building Notice or Full Plans application must be submitted, and the work is inspected at key stages. Completion of the work is evidenced by a completion certificate. Where an Approved Inspector is used, the local authority is notified via an Initial Notice.

See also: Building Regulations

Specification

Specification Clause
A specification clause is a written description of the required quality, standards, and workmanship for a building element, forming part of the contract documents. For roofing, a specification clause for Metrotile steel tiles would include: product description and profile reference; BBA Certificate number; relevant standards (BS 5534, BS EN 14782); installer qualifications; underlay type and fixing; batten specification and fixing schedule; valley, ridge, hip, verge, and abutment details; and maintenance instructions. Specification clauses are typically written in NBS format (using NBS Plus or NBS Chorus) and form a legally binding contract requirement. Metrotile provides manufacturer specification clauses via NBS Plus.

See also: NBS Plus · Proprietary Specification

Proprietary Specification
A proprietary specification names a specific manufacturer and product (e.g. "Metrotile Shingle profile, ZAM®-coated steel, colour: Slate Grey") rather than defining performance requirements and leaving the product open to substitution. Proprietary specifications are used when the architect or client has a specific reason to require a particular product — its aesthetic, its BBA certification, its manufacturer warranty, or its established performance record. Under UK standard forms of contract (JCT, NEC), a proprietary specification can be complied with only by supply of the named product (no approved equivalent substitution unless the specification includes an 'or approved equivalent' clause). Specifiers should state 'no approved equivalent' explicitly if they wish to prevent substitution.

See also: Performance Specification · Specification Clause

Performance Specification
A performance specification defines the required outcomes (weight, U-value, fire classification, wind resistance, guarantee period) rather than naming a specific product, allowing multiple manufacturers to bid with compliant alternatives. This approach is common in public-sector and framework procurement to promote competition. Metrotile products are frequently submitted as compliant alternatives against performance specifications written around Class A fire, BBA or ETA certification, ≤8 kg/m² installed weight, BS 5534 wind resistance, and 40-year manufacturer guarantee. Specifiers should include product sample approval and material data submission requirements to ensure that the accepted alternative genuinely meets the performance brief.

See also: Proprietary Specification · Sample Approval

NBS Plus
NBS Plus is the manufacturer specification service operated by NBS (National Building Specification), now part of Byggfakta Group. It provides manufacturers with a standardised route to make their product specification data available to architects and specifiers through the NBS Chorus platform — the leading cloud-based construction specification tool used in the UK. When a specifier selects a Metrotile product within NBS Chorus, the NBS Plus content automatically populates the relevant specification clause with the correct product description, standard references, and installation requirements, reducing specification errors and saving the specifier time. NBS Plus clauses are reviewed and updated by the manufacturer and reviewed by NBS editors for accuracy.

See also: Specification Clause · NBS National BIM Library

Sample Approval
Sample approval is the process by which the architect, client, or planner reviews and formally approves a physical sample of the proposed roofing material before it is ordered and installed. For Metrotile tiles, sample approval involves Metrotile supplying a quantity of the specified tile in the chosen profile and colour (e.g. 3–5 tiles), which the design team inspects against the specification and the visual requirements of the planning consent (particularly in conservation areas and listed building settings). Once approved and signed off, the sample typically becomes the benchmark for the delivered product — tiles must match it in profile, colour, and finish. Sample approval is a standard contractor submission requirement in most JCT and NEC contracts.

See also: Proprietary Specification

Schedule of Works
A schedule of works is a document that lists all the work items to be carried out on a project — typically in order of trade or construction sequence — forming the basis for tender pricing and programme planning. In roofing, a schedule of works for a re-roofing project would include: stripping existing tiles (or overlay preparation); structural inspection and repair; underlay installation; counter-batten and batten installation; tile fixing (field tiles); ridge, hip, valley, verge, and flashing installation; guttering and downpipes; and snagging and final inspection. The schedule of works is used by the contractor to prepare a bill of quantities or a lump-sum price, and by the contract administrator to value interim payment applications.

Synonyms: Works schedule · Scope of works

BIM & Digital

BIM (Building Information Modelling)
Building Information Modelling (BIM) is a digital collaborative process for designing, constructing, and managing buildings using intelligent 3D models that carry data about every component — geometry, materials, manufacturer data, performance characteristics, warranty details, and maintenance requirements. The UK government mandated BIM Level 2 on centrally procured public-sector projects from April 2016 (BS EN ISO 19650 is the international standard). BIM enables clash detection (identifying conflicts between structure, services, and envelope in the model before construction), quantity take-off automation, sustainability analysis, and asset data management through the building's life. Metrotile supports BIM through provision of Revit families, IFC files, and NBS-linked product data for its tile range.

Synonyms: Building Information Management · Digital design

See also: Revit · IFC · COBie

Autodesk Revit
Autodesk Revit is the leading BIM authoring software in the UK and global construction industry, used by architects, structural engineers, and MEP engineers to create parametric building models. Revit uses 'families' — parametric objects that contain 3D geometry and data parameters — for building components including roofing tiles. Metrotile provides Revit families for its principal tile profiles, allowing architects to place, array, and document tiles within their Revit model with accurate geometry, weight data, and product parameters. Revit models are exported to IFC format for exchange with non-Revit collaborators and to COBie for asset data handover to the building owner or facilities manager.

See also: BIM · IFC

IFC (Industry Foundation Classes)
IFC (Industry Foundation Classes) is an open, vendor-neutral data schema for building and infrastructure information, published by buildingSMART International and standardised as ISO 16739. IFC files allow BIM models to be exchanged between different software platforms (Revit, ArchiCAD, Tekla, Bentley, etc.) without loss of geometry or data, enabling multi-discipline collaboration without mandating a single proprietary software tool. UK BIM Level 2 mandates the use of IFC for information exchange on public-sector projects. Metrotile tile data is available in IFC format for import into any IFC-compliant BIM authoring tool, enabling specifiers to incorporate accurate product geometry and properties into their federated BIM models.

Synonyms: Open BIM · buildingSMART

See also: BIM · Revit

COBie
COBie (Construction Operations Building Information Exchange) is a data schema for capturing and exchanging information about building components and systems during design and construction, for handover to the building owner and facilities manager at practical completion. COBie data is typically delivered as a structured spreadsheet (Excel or database) and includes: component type, manufacturer, model, serial number, warranty period, and maintenance requirements. For roofing, COBie data would include Metrotile tile type, BBA Certificate reference, installation date, guarantee period (40 years), and maintenance schedule. COBie handover is required on government-funded BIM projects and is increasingly requested on privately funded schemes.

See also: BIM

NBS National BIM Library
The NBS National BIM Library is a free online repository of BIM objects (Revit families and IFC files) for construction products, published by NBS and meeting the NBS BIM Object Standard. It is the most widely used source of BIM objects for UK specifiers and is integrated with NBS Chorus, so that selecting a product's BIM object in the library can automatically link to its NBS specification clause. Metrotile tile profiles are available on the NBS National BIM Library, allowing architects to download accurately modelled, data-rich tile objects that carry the product parameters needed for BIM coordination and COBie handover without requiring manual data entry.

See also: BIM · NBS Plus

Procurement & People

RIBA Plan of Work
The RIBA Plan of Work is the standard project management framework published by the Royal Institute of British Architects, organising the process of briefing, designing, constructing, and operating a building into eight sequential stages (0–7): Strategic Definition (0), Preparation & Brief (1), Concept Design (2), Spatial Coordination (3), Technical Design (4), Manufacturing & Construction (5), Handover (6), and Use (7). For roofing specification, product selection is typically confirmed at Stage 3–4; detailed specification clauses, fixing designs, and junction details are produced at Stage 4 (Technical Design); and the product is ordered and installed at Stage 5 (Construction). Metrotile's technical team can provide specification support at any RIBA stage.

See also: Specification Clause

Architect
An architect is a registered design professional — in the UK, the title 'architect' is protected by the Architects Act 1997 and administered by the Architects Registration Board (ARB); most practising architects are also members of the Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA). The architect is typically the lead designer on a building project, responsible for: obtaining planning consent; coordinating the structural engineer, services engineers, and other consultants; writing the specification; and administering the building contract on behalf of the client. Architects are a primary audience for Metrotile's technical specification service: the company's specification team works directly with architects to select the appropriate profile, develop junction details, and produce NBS specification clauses.

See also: RIBA Plan of Work

Main Contractor & Sub-Contractor
The main contractor is the company that holds the principal contract with the building owner for delivery of the construction works, managing the programme, site logistics, and the network of specialist sub-contractors. Roofing sub-contractors (roofing contractors) are the specialist tradespeople who install the roof covering under the main contractor's programme. For Metrotile installations, Metrotile recommends and supports a national network of approved roofing contractors who are trained in the system and its installation requirements. Working with a Metrotile-approved installer ensures that the BBA Certificate and manufacturer warranty conditions are met and that any warranty claims are processed efficiently.

See also: Constructionline

Constructionline
Constructionline is the UK's leading pre-qualification and supply chain management service, used by public-sector bodies and large private clients to pre-qualify and manage contractors and suppliers. Members complete a standardised pre-qualification process covering financial health, insurance, health and safety, environmental policy, and equality. Constructionline membership is required for contractors and suppliers wishing to work on many public-sector and housing association schemes. Metrotile is a Constructionline-registered supplier, enabling public-sector procurement teams and main contractors to confirm Metrotile's compliance status without conducting their own detailed assessment.

See also: Framework Agreement

Framework Agreement
A framework agreement is a long-term procurement arrangement between a client (or group of clients) and one or more approved suppliers or contractors, establishing the terms and conditions under which individual call-off contracts can be placed without the need for a new competitive tender for each project. Social housing providers, local authorities, and NHS Trusts typically procure roofing via frameworks such as Procurement for Housing (PfH), Fusion21, YPO, or Crown Commercial Service (CCS). Metrotile products are available through several public-sector procurement frameworks, enabling housing associations and councils to call off Metrotile re-roofing projects directly against pre-agreed pricing, specification, and terms.

See also: Procurement Route · Constructionline

Procurement Route
Procurement route describes the contractual and organisational arrangement chosen by the client for delivering a construction project — determining who designs the works, who takes the risk, and how the contractor is paid. Common procurement routes in UK construction include: Traditional (Design-Bid-Build) — architect designs, then contractor builds to that design, lowest risk for design quality; Design & Build — a single contractor takes design and construction responsibility, transferring risk to the contractor; Management Contracting — a management contractor organises specialist trades; and Two-Stage Tendering — contractor involved early in design development before a lump-sum price is agreed. For roofing, the procurement route determines at what stage Metrotile can be specified (in traditional procurement, at design stage; in D&B, the contractor may select the tile subject to an employer's requirements).

See also: Framework Agreement

CDM Regulations
The Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 (CDM 2015) set out the health and safety duties of all parties involved in a construction project — client, Principal Designer, Principal Contractor, designers, and contractors. CDM applies to all construction work, including maintenance and re-roofing. Key roofing health and safety hazards covered by CDM include: working at height (roof edge protection, scaffolding, fall arrest systems); manual handling of materials; exposure to asbestos-containing materials during stripping of pre-2000 roofs; and safe storage and handling of tiles during installation. The Principal Contractor must provide a Construction Phase Plan and implement safe systems of work; the Principal Designer must ensure that CDM risks are considered during design and specification.

See also: Contractor
